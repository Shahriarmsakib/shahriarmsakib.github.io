    Robustness Evaluation for EMG-Based Pose Estimation Models under Controlled Perturbations
    Shahriar M. Sakib and others
    Submitted, HRI 2027 Short Contributions , Oct 2026
    Manuscript currently under anonymous peer review.
    Abs Bib Code

    Developed a lightweight stress-testing toolkit for EMG pose estimation models using controlled perturbations such as additive noise, channel dropout, amplitude scaling, and temporal shift. The work compares model failure profiles under changing sensing conditions and studies robustness considerations for downstream human robot interaction.

  2. Heliyon
    Enhancing breast cancer classification via histopathological image analysis: Leveraging self-supervised contrastive learning and transfer learning
    Faisal Bin Ashraf, S. M. Maksudul Alam, and Shahriar M. Sakib
    Heliyon, 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML Code

    We classify benign and malignant breast-cancer histopathology images from the BreakHis dataset at four magnification levels using two approaches. The first is a self-supervised contrastive scheme that starts from a small labeled subset and progressively adds confidently pseudo-labeled images. The second is a lightweight architecture that combines the early stages of ResNet50 with a naive Inception block, trained with several transfer-learning schedules. The hybrid reaches 98% accuracy at 40X and 200X and 94% at 100X and 400X with about 3.6 million parameters, less than half the size of the leading prior models in our comparison.

  3. HSJ
    Basin-wide groundwater level forecasting in Kumamoto, Japan: integrating transfer learning with Long Short-term Memory Network
    A. T. M. Sakiur Rahman, Shahriar M. Sakib, Md Kaykobad Reza, and 4 more authors
    Hydrological Sciences Journal, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML Code

    Deep learning models forecast groundwater levels accurately, but they are usually trained and tuned separately for every observation well, which does not scale to a whole basin. We pre-train a single LSTM on the basin-average groundwater series of the Kumamoto Groundwater Basin, Japan, and transfer it to each of seven observation wells by fine-tuning only its last layers (TL-LSTM). Compared with independently trained per-well LSTMs, the transferred models reach similar accuracy for one-, two- and three-month-ahead forecasts while needing far less training and calibration effort, making them practical for large-scale groundwater forecasting.
